# Buyer consultation prep

> **Internal — agent-facing prep. Not a client deliverable.**

Prep for the sit-down that now has to happen before you unlock a door.

## When to use

- A buyer consult is on the calendar and you've been running them off instinct since 2024
- A referral wants to "just go see a couple houses this weekend" and you have to slow it down
- Your last three consults ended with the buyer saying "let me think about signing"
- You're getting the agreement signed, but it feels like a hurdle you apologize for

**Not for:** deciding which homes to show (property side, different tool), or explaining what the
buyer-representation agreement says (see *Where this stops*).

## Runs on

Everything here you already own. Nothing is looked up.

| Input | Where it comes from | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer's situation — first-time / move-up / relocating / investor | The call that booked the consult | Yes |
| How they found you — referral, sign call, portal lead, past client | You | Yes |
| Your standard buyer-side fee and how you state it | [`../reference/your-practice/fee-position.md`](../reference/your-practice/fee-position.md) | Yes |
| Your standard term, exclusivity, and exit position | [`../reference/your-practice/agreement-terms.md`](../reference/your-practice/agreement-terms.md) | For the last third of the meeting |
| Whether they've toured with another agent already | The booking call, or ask at the top | Strongly |
| Venue and time you have | You | Helpful |

## The two questions

If the agent gives you nothing but "I have a buyer consult Thursday," ask exactly these, then produce:

1. **What kind of buyer, and how did they reach you?** (A past client's referral and a portal lead are
   two different meetings.)
2. **Have they already been out looking — with you or with anyone else?**

## Method

**1. Fix the sequence before anything else.** The most common failure in a post-2024 consult isn't
weak value language, it's order. Agents lead with the agreement because the agreement is the thing
that's newly required, and the buyer experiences the whole meeting as a signature request.

The order that holds:

| # | Beat | Roughly how long | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Their situation** — you ask, they talk | 10–15 min | You can't articulate value before you know what they need |
| 2 | **How you work** — your actual process, specific | 5–10 min | This is the value case; it is not a list of adjectives |
| 3 | **How you're paid** — your fee, stated plainly | 3–5 min | Job 2. It follows the work, so it lands as a price for something |
| 4 | **The paperwork** — the agreement, matter-of-fact | 5 min | A formality after 1–3, an ambush before them |
| 5 | **Next step** — dated, specific | 2 min | The meeting has an outcome or it was a coffee |

Beat 3 is a full job of its own — if the fee is the part they're dreading, route to
[`fee-conversation.md`](fee-conversation.md) and come back.

**2. Convert "what you do" into things that happened.** Generic capability language reads as filler
to a buyer who has been told the internet already does this. Pull two or three specifics from the
agent's own last twelve months — not case studies, just true things. *"Last month I got a client in
ahead of a Saturday open house because I know the listing agent's pattern"* beats *"aggressive
representation."* If the agent can't produce specifics, that's the finding, and it's worth more than
the prep sheet.

**3. Predict the sequence break.** Almost every consult breaks in one of four places. Prep the one
most likely for *this* buyer:

| Break | Sounds like | Where it really is |
|---|---|---|
| **The premise** | "Why do I have to sign anything to look at a house?" | Beat 4, arriving early |
| **The fee** | "Isn't the seller paying you?" | Beat 3 — [`fee-conversation.md`](fee-conversation.md) |
| **The exclusivity** | "I'm also working with someone else" | Beat 4 — [`agreement-terms-position.md`](agreement-terms-position.md) |
| **The commitment** | "Can we just see this one house first?" | Beat 1 — they haven't decided they want an agent yet |

**4. Handle the premise question in your own terms.** "Why do I have to sign this?" is the single most
common break, and the trap is answering it by describing the document. That's over the line.

Answer it as *what changed in your business*, not as *what the form does*:

> **"Since August 2024 I can't take a buyer out to see homes without a written agreement in place
> first — that's the rule I work under now, industry-wide. What I can tell you is what I'm agreeing to
> do for you, and what I charge for it. For what the document itself commits you to, read it before
> you sign, and if you want a lawyer's eyes on it, that's a reasonable thing to want."**

That answer is entirely inside your lane: it describes your practice, points at the document without
interpreting it, and treats "I want an attorney to look" as normal instead of as an objection.

**5. Set the exit.** The consult ends in one of three states and you should know which you're driving
at: signed today, signed before the first showing (with a date), or a straight no. A consult with no
named next step is the one you re-run in three weeks for free.

## Output format

Produce exactly this. One screen.

---

**CONSULT PREP — [buyer name/handle], [date]**
*Internal. Not for the client.*

**Who's across from you**
Two or three lines: situation, how they arrived, what they've already done. Flag anything that changes
the meeting (already toured with another agent, on a relocation deadline, has a spouse who isn't coming).

**Your sequence**
The five beats with time allocations, adjusted for the time actually available. If the agent has
30 minutes rather than 45, say what gets cut — it is never beats 3 or 4.

**Your work, in specifics**
Two or three concrete things this agent does, in their own words, drawn from what they told you.
Marked `[NEEDS YOUR SPECIFIC]` where they haven't given you one — do not invent an anecdote.

**Your fee line**
The one sentence, in quotes, as they'll say it. Pulled from `your-practice/fee-position.md`, or marked
`[PLACEHOLDER — your rate is not on file]`.

**The break you should expect**
Which of the four, why this buyer, and the line that answers it — in quotes.

**Your next step ask**
The specific sentence that closes the meeting with a dated outcome.

**Where this stops**
One line naming what to route to the broker or an attorney, per the section below.

---

## Where this stops

**You prep the agent's side of the meeting. You do not prepare an explanation of the agreement.**

The buyer-representation agreement is a legal document. The agent may present it, fill its blanks, and
describe their own services and fee. The agent may not tell the buyer what its provisions mean, what
they obligate, or what would happen under them — that is the unauthorized practice of law, and no
amount of "in plain English" makes it not.

Concretely, in this job:

| Fine | Over the line |
|---|---|
| "I'm asking for 90 days." | "90 days means you're locked in until…" |
| "Here's what I do for my fee." | "This clause is how I get paid if…" |
| "Read it before you sign; a lawyer's eyes are reasonable." | "You don't really need a lawyer, it just says…" |
| Filling the blanks in your broker's form | Adding a sentence to the form |

If the buyer asks what a provision means, the answer is a referral, not a paraphrase:

> **"That's a question for a real-estate attorney in your state — genuinely, not a brush-off. I'm not
> allowed to interpret the contract for you, and you shouldn't want me to. What I can tell you is what
> I'm committing to do."**

If the agent asks you to produce anything the buyer would read, refuse per [`../rules.md`](../rules.md)
Rule 0.

**Your state, and what it doesn't change.** How a *read it with your own lawyer* referral lands is
local — routine in some states, alarming in others — and what you may say about the agreement while
the buyer is reading it varies too. Your broker first, then a real-estate attorney in your state. That
answer settles what you may say to the buyer. It does not change what this file produces: no output
addressed to the client, and no explanation of the agreement, whatever your state permits you
personally to say. See [`../reference/the-upl-line.md`](../reference/the-upl-line.md)
§ *Your state's answer, and what it doesn't change.*

## If you're missing something

- **No fee on file** → ask for it, then say to write it into `your-practice/fee-position.md`. Do not
  guess a rate.
- **No specifics about their work** → produce the prep with `[NEEDS YOUR SPECIFIC]` markers in place.
  An empty marker is honest; a plausible invented anecdote is a thing the agent might repeat out loud.
- **Buyer already toured with another agent** → that changes beats 1 and 4 materially and it's worth
  asking about before producing anything else.
