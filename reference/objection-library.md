# Objection library

Twelve objections that arrive constantly, each built the same way: **what's true in it → the answer →
the return to the number → what not to say.**

**How to use these:** not as scripts. Every line here needs to come out in the agent's own vocabulary
or it will sound rehearsed, which is worse than sounding uncertain. Take the structure, drop the words
that aren't yours.

**Two rules that apply to all twelve.**

1. **Concede what's true first.** Every objection below contains something real. Agreeing with it costs
   nothing and buys the rest of the sentence. An agent who argues with the true part loses the room.
2. **End on the number.** An objection response that trails off has conceded. Return to the position
   before you stop talking.

---

## Buyer-side — the fee

### 1. "Isn't the seller paying you?"

**True in it:** Often, yes — roughly 70% of 2025 transactions still included an offer of buyer-agent
compensation ([`compensation-landscape.md`](compensation-landscape.md)). The buyer isn't being naive.

> **"A lot of the time, yes — and I'll ask for that on every house we write on. What changed is that
> my fee is now something you and I agree on up front, rather than something that just appears at
> closing. So: my fee is 2.5%. In practice I ask the seller's side to cover it, and usually they do.
> If they come back with less, we talk about the gap before we write anything."**

**Don't say:** "The seller pays, don't worry about it." It isn't reliably true, it defers the
conversation to the worst possible moment — mid-offer — and it makes the agent look either uninformed
or evasive when the gap appears.

### 2. "The other agent said they'd do it for 2%."

**True in it:** Someone did quote them that. Fee is negotiable and always was.

> **"They might well be worth it — I'm not going to run down another agent I don't know. What I can
> tell you is what you get from me, which is [two specifics]. My fee is 2.5%. If price is the deciding
> factor, they're the better call, and I'd genuinely rather you go with the person you trust than have
> you feel talked into me."**

**Don't say:** anything about the other agent's competence. It reads as insecurity and the buyer will
repeat it. **Don't** discount on the spot either — a rate that moves the first time it's questioned
tells the buyer it will move again, which guarantees a second attempt.

### 3. "You're taking a percentage of what I spend — so you make more if I overpay?"

**True in it:** Structurally, yes, on the buyer side. This is a real misalignment and the sharpest
buyers spot it.

> **"That's a fair read of how percentage works, and it's the honest weakness of the structure. Here's
> why it doesn't play out that way in practice: [the agent's own reason]. If it bothers you, I'll do
> this as a flat fee instead — the number's the same to me, and it takes the incentive question off
> the table entirely."**

**Don't say:** "I'd never do that." A character defense doesn't answer a structural objection. The
structure is the answer — this is the one objection where switching to flat fee is a genuinely strong
move rather than a concession. See [`../skills/fee-conversation.md`](../skills/fee-conversation.md)
step 3.

### 4. "I found the house myself on Zillow."

**True in it:** They did. Search stopped being the agent's value in about 2012.

> **"You did, and honestly, finding them is the part that got easy. What I'm charging for starts after
> that: [what the agent actually does — the offer, the inspection read, the negotiation, the failure
> modes they've seen]. If all you needed was the address, you wouldn't need me, and I'd tell you that."**

**Don't say:** anything implying the portal data is unreliable. The buyer has used it, it worked, and
the claim makes the agent sound like the past.

### 5. "Can you cut your fee to help me close the gap?" *(late, mid-deal)*

**True in it:** The gap is real and the agent is the only party in the room whose money is discretionary.

> **"I understand why I'm the one you're asking — I'm the flexible number in the deal. Before I touch
> it, let's look at [the other levers]. If we get to the end and it's genuinely the last thing between
> you and the house, come back to me and we'll talk. But I don't want to spend my fee first when there
> are two other places to find that money."**

**Don't say:** yes immediately. This is the request most likely to be an opening rather than a
conclusion, and the agent who concedes first gets asked again at closing.

## Buyer-side — the agreement

### 6. "Why do I have to sign anything just to look at a house?"

Handled in full in [`what-changed-2024.md`](what-changed-2024.md) § *Answering the client who
challenges the premise*, because the answer has to stay clear of describing the document.

**Short form:** name the rule you work under, say what you commit to, point at the document without
interpreting it, treat "I want a lawyer to read it" as reasonable.

**Don't say:** "It just says you'll work with me" — that's interpreting the agreement, and it's wrong.
See [`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md).

### 7. "I'm also talking to another agent."

**True in it:** They are. Nothing stops them.

> **"That's fine — you should talk to a couple. Let's do this on the properties I show you rather than
> the whole search, and if you decide you want me running all of it, we'll talk then."**

**Don't say:** anything about loyalty. This is a *scope* problem with a scope answer — shrink the ask.
See [`../skills/agreement-terms-position.md`](../skills/agreement-terms-position.md) step 3.

### 8. "Can we just see this one house first?"

**True in it:** They haven't decided they want an agent yet, which is a reasonable place to be.

> **"We can — let's do a short agreement covering this one, and you can decide about the rest after
> you've seen how I work."**

**Don't say:** no. The short-scope agreement exists exactly for this, and a buyer who is told "sign for
six months or I can't show you anything" hears an ultimatum in the first ten minutes.

### 9. "Six months? That feels like a lot."

**True in it:** It is a lot, from a stranger, in a first meeting.

> **"Agreed — let's do 30 days. If you like how I work we'll extend it. I'd rather earn that than ask
> for it today."**

**Don't say:** a justification for six months. If the agent can win the objection by conceding the
thing that costs them least, the concession is the better play, and it buys goodwill on the fee.

## Seller-side

### 10. "Why would I pay the buyer's agent?"

**True in it:** It's their money, and the framing they've absorbed makes it sound like a donation.

> **"It's not really 'paying their agent' — it's the same question as a closing-cost credit or a rate
> buydown. Are you willing to use some of your proceeds to widen the pool of buyers who can actually
> write on this house? Sometimes the answer's no. It depends who your buyer is."**

**Don't say:** "You have to or nobody will show it." Overstated, and the seller will find out. Full
treatment in
[`../skills/seller-compensation-decision.md`](../skills/seller-compensation-decision.md).

### 11. "I heard I don't have to pay them anymore."

**True in it:** Entirely true — and it was true before 2024 as well.

> **"Correct, and you never actually had to. What changed is that it's now a decision you make
> deliberately instead of something that happened automatically. Most sellers are still choosing to
> offer something — but it's a choice now, and it's yours."**

**Don't say:** "Well, technically…" The seller is right. Confirm it, then move to the decision.

### 12. "What do other sellers around here do?"

**True in it:** A completely natural question.

**This is a trap, and it's the most important entry in this file.** Answering it with a rate — even
truthfully, even hedged — hands the seller a "standard," and representing compensation as standard or
customary is precisely the conduct the 2024 practice changes came out of. It is also, for most agents,
an answer they don't actually have.

> **"There's no standard — it's negotiable, and it always was. I see a range, and I'm wary of quoting
> you a number as if it were a going rate, because that's the thing the industry just spent years in
> court over. What I'd rather do is tell you what I'd recommend for *this* house and why."**

**Don't say:** "Most people around here do 2.5%." Don't say it even if the agent believes it. See
[`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md) § *One more boundary*, and Rule 1 in
[`../rules.md`](../rules.md) — this repo does not hold local rate data and will not estimate it.

## The pattern under all twelve

Every objection in this file is answered by **conceding the true part and returning to the position**.
None of them is answered by arguing, by discounting, or by explaining the paperwork.

If an agent is facing an objection not in this list, that's the structure to build with — and if the
objection is *"what does this clause mean,"* it isn't an objection at all. It's a referral. See
[`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md).
