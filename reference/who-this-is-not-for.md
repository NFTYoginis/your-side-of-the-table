# Who this is not for

Declining out loud, so nobody adopts this and discovers the mismatch in week three.

---

## Not for these

**1. Commercial real estate.** Different transaction structure, different fee conventions, different
counterparties, and the 2024 residential practice changes don't govern it. Every worked example, every
objection, and every position in this repo assumes a residential deal with a consumer on the other
side. Nothing here transfers cleanly, and a repo that claimed it did would be lying about its range.

**2. Agents whose brokerage sets the position.** If your brokerage mandates the fee, the term, the
exclusivity, and the compensation posture — and some do — then jobs 2, 3, and 4 are asking you to
decide things that aren't yours to decide. Job 1 and job 5 still work. That's two jobs, not five, and
you should know that before you adopt it.

**3. Anyone wanting contract language.** Not a partial fit, not a "mostly." This repo refuses to draft,
alter, or interpret any form, and it refuses it consistently enough to be annoying if that's what you
came for. [`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md).

**4. Anyone wanting market data.** No MLS, no comps, no local rates, no estimates.
[`no-listing-data.md`](no-listing-data.md). If the question is *what's it worth* or *what does my market
do*, this is the wrong repo and it will say so every time you ask.

**5. Consumers.** Buyers and sellers, this isn't for you. Every file is written to the agent, about
their side. Reading it will tell you what your agent is preparing, which is a strange way to prepare
for a conversation with them and a bad substitute for asking them directly.

**6. Anyone who wants a script to read.** Positions hold under improvisation; scripts break the moment
the client says something off-book. If you want a word-for-word track to recite, you'll find this
repo's insistence on reasoning-before-line to be overhead.

## The honest edges

**Job 5 fits loosest.** Post-inspection strategy isn't strictly a creation of the 2024 practice
changes, and a stricter reading of this repo's scope would drop it. It's here because it's the same
shape — the agent taking a position, close to paperwork they must not touch — and because the UPL
boundary matters more there than anywhere else in the deal. Reasoning in
[`what-changed-2024.md`](what-changed-2024.md).

**This is a US repo.** The practice changes, the UPL framing, and the compensation conventions are all
United States. Agents elsewhere will find the structure recognisable and the specifics wrong.

**State variation is real and unhandled.** Attorney-state closing practice, promulgated-form regimes,
and state-specific licensee rules all shift what an agent may do. This repo takes the strict reading
everywhere, which is safe but is not tailored. Your broker and your state commission outrank every
file here.

## If you're a fit

You're a licensed US residential agent who decides your own fee and your own terms, you've been
improvising these conversations since August 2024, and you'd like ten minutes of preparation before
the next one. Start at [`../README.md`](../README.md).
