# What changed, and why these conversations exist

Context for the agent, and for the moment a client challenges the premise. Two facts do most of the work.

---

## The two practice changes

Implemented **August 17, 2024**, across the country, following the National Association of REALTORS®
settlement agreement resolving claims brought on behalf of home sellers over broker commissions:

**1. A written agreement before touring.** An agent working with a buyer must enter into a written
buyer agreement before the buyer tours a home. Those agreements must include a **specific and
conspicuous disclosure of the amount or rate of compensation** the agent will receive, or how it will
be determined.

**2. Offers of compensation off the MLS.** Offers of compensation are prohibited on Multiple Listing
Services. They remain available to consumers **off-MLS**, through negotiation and consultation.

Sources:

- [NAR — *Reminds Members and Consumers of Real Estate Practice Change Implementation on August 17, 2024*](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/national-association-of-realtors-reminds-members-and-consumers-of-real-estate-practice-change)
- [NAR — *Final Reminder of NAR Practice Change Implementation*](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/national-association-of-realtors-provides-final-reminder-of-august-17-nar-practice-change-implementation)
- [NAR — Settlement FAQs](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs)
- [NAR — *What the NAR Settlement Means for Home Buyers and Sellers*](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers)

**One clarification worth carrying**, because agents get it wrong in front of clients: the practice
changes **do not require an agency agreement and do not dictate any type of relationship.** The
requirement is a *written agreement with compensation disclosed* before touring — not a particular
representation model. If an agent tells a buyer "you have to become my client to see a house," they've
overstated it.

## Why five conversations came out of two rules

The rules are narrow. The consequences aren't, because both of them move a number from *implicit* to
*spoken*.

| Before | After | Conversation it created |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers toured first, paperwork came later or never | Written agreement, compensation disclosed, before the first door | **Job 1** — buyer consultation prep |
| Buyer-side compensation sat on the MLS; the buyer rarely heard a number from their own agent | The agent states their own fee, in the first meeting, out loud | **Job 2** — the fee conversation |
| Sellers offered compensation as a near-automatic listing input | A seller decision, made deliberately, off-MLS | **Job 3** — seller compensation decision |
| Term and exclusivity were whatever the form said | The agent is proposing terms to someone deciding whether to sign at all | **Job 4** — agreement terms position |
| Repair requests happened after a relationship had already formed over weeks of touring | The relationship is contractual from day one, and asks land differently | **Job 5** — post-inspection position |

Job 5 is the loosest fit and it belongs anyway: the same shift that made the agent's compensation
explicit made every ask in the deal more explicit. Agents who could once carry a negotiation on
accumulated goodwill now do it on a footing that was formalized in week one.

## Answering the client who challenges the premise

The most common version, from a buyer: **"Why do I have to sign something just to look at a house?"**

The answer stays on the agent's side of the line — it describes the rule the agent works under and
what the agent commits to, and it points at the document without interpreting it:

> **"Since August 2024 I can't take a buyer out to see homes without a written agreement in place
> first — that's industry-wide now, not a house rule. What I can tell you is what I'm agreeing to do
> for you and what I charge. For what the document commits *you* to, read it before you sign, and if
> you'd like an attorney to look at it, that's a reasonable thing to want."**

What that answer deliberately doesn't do: explain the agreement. See
[`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md).

A seller version — *"I heard I don't have to pay the buyer's agent anymore"* — is a true statement
that was also true before, and it belongs in
[`../skills/seller-compensation-decision.md`](../skills/seller-compensation-decision.md), where it's a
decision rather than a correction.

## What did not change

Worth knowing, because agents over-claim in both directions:

- **Compensation was always negotiable.** The settlement didn't make it negotiable; it ended practices
  that obscured that it already was. An agent who says "it's negotiable now" is conceding a point that
  was never true.
- **Sellers may still offer buyer-agent compensation.** Off-MLS, through negotiation. It is not banned
  and it did not disappear — see [`compensation-landscape.md`](compensation-landscape.md).
- **Nothing set, capped, or standardized any rate.** There is no standard, there is no customary
  number, and representing one is the exact conduct the litigation was about.
