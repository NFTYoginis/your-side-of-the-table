# Compensation landscape — dated national context, not your number

**Read the warning before the numbers.** This file exists so an agent has a rough national frame in
their head. It does not exist to answer "what should I charge" or "what do sellers offer around here."
Under [`../rules.md`](../rules.md) Rule 2, nothing here may be applied to a local market, presented as
current, or described as standard.

---

## Two figures, two provenances

Everything below comes from one of two places, and they are labelled, because they do not fully agree.

- **[BRIEF]** — supplied in the operator's build brief, 2026-08-19. Not independently verified here.
- **[PUBLIC]** — from public secondary sources retrieved 2026-08-19, listed at the bottom.

### Seller offers of buyer-agent compensation

| Figure | Provenance | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **~70% of early-2025 transactions still included an offer of buyer-agent compensation** | **[BRIEF]** | |
| **~70% of 2025 transactions** — sellers offering buyer-agent compensation remained the most common arrangement | **[PUBLIC]** | Independently consistent with the brief |

**The two agree, and this is the single most useful number in the file.** It answers the seller who
believes offering compensation ended in 2024, and it answers the buyer's agent who has started assuming
they'll be paid by their client. Neither is what the market did. It changed from automatic to
decided — and most sellers, having decided, still offer.

## The divergence, stated plainly

**The buyer-side figures broadly agree.** The brief's 2–2.5% and the public 2.3–2.5% / 2.42% describe
the same band.

**The totals do not.** The brief's **~5.5–5.7%** sits meaningfully above the public **~5.0–5.2%** — a
gap of roughly half a point, which on a $500,000 sale is about $2,500. This repo does not resolve the
disagreement, and does not average the two into a fake consensus.

Plausible explanations, none confirmed: different measurement windows (early-2025 versus late-2025, in
a period where rates were moving), different transaction samples, different treatment of deals where
one side was unrepresented, or ordinary methodological divergence between commercial datasets.

**How to hold it:** the *direction* is well supported — total commissions drifted down after August
2024, buyer-side more than listing-side, and the drift is measured in tenths of a point, not in the
collapse some coverage predicted. The *level* is uncertain to about half a point nationally, and by
much more than that in any single market.

## Why the aggregates are weaker than they look

Four reasons to distrust any national commission number, including the ones above:

1. **They're national.** Local variation exceeds the national year-over-year change by a wide margin.
   A national figure that moved 0.3 points tells you almost nothing about a market that runs a point
   off the national average in either direction.
2. **They go stale fast**, and this file does not update itself. Everything here is a 2026-08-19
   snapshot of figures already describing 2024–2025.
3. **The sources are secondary.** The public figures came from commercial real-estate content sites
   summarizing datasets, not from reading the underlying research. Methodologies differ and are rarely
   stated in the summaries.
4. **Averages hide the distribution.** "Buyer-side averages 2.42%" is consistent with a market where a
   large share of transactions are at 2.5% and a growing tail is at zero. The average moves; the mode
   may not have.

## What to do with this file

**Legitimate uses:**

- Orienting an agent who has lost the plot on whether compensation offers still happen (they do, ~70%)
- Sanity-checking a client's claim that "nobody pays buyer agents anymore"
- Noting the direction of travel, with the date attached

**Illegitimate uses — refuse these:**

- "What should I charge?" → Not answerable from here. See
  [`../skills/fee-conversation.md`](../skills/fee-conversation.md); the number is the agent's.
- "What do sellers offer in my area?" → Not in this file and not obtainable by this repo. Rule 1,
  [`no-listing-data.md`](no-listing-data.md).
- "Is 2.5% standard?" → **There is no standard.** Saying otherwise is the antitrust-adjacent claim the
  2024 changes came out of. See [`the-upl-line.md`](the-upl-line.md) § *One more boundary*.

**The file that outranks this one:**
[`your-practice/what-i-see-locally.md`](your-practice/what-i-see-locally.md). An agent's own
observations of their own market — what they've actually seen offered, accepted, and refused — beat
every number on this page, and when the two disagree the agent's file wins. That's not modesty about
the data. It's that the agent is the only party to this conversation with local, current, first-hand
observation.
